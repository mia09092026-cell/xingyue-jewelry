Necklaces are a display category. The pendant has to sit well, the chain has to match the price point, and the length has to work for the customer who is buying it. A photo can show the stone clearly and still hide the details that decide whether the necklace sells: chain weight, clasp type, pendant drop, plating and stone setting.

This guide covers the practical questions behind a wholesale moissanite necklace order. Current ready-stock styles are on the [wholesale moissanite necklaces page](/wholesale/moissanite-necklaces).

## Start with the necklace type

Most wholesale moissanite necklaces fall into two groups:

- **Pendant necklaces** — one main stone or a small cluster, usually on a fine chain. The pendant is the focal point.
- **Station necklaces** — several stones placed along the chain. The look depends on spacing, stone size and chain length.

Pendant styles are easier to plan around a size and price range. Station styles need more attention to spacing and stone matching, because the eye sees the whole chain at once. Ask which type the SKU is and whether the pendant is fixed or removable.

## Choose the chain length before the pendant

A pendant that looks balanced at 16 inches may look lost at 20 inches. Chain length changes the drop point, the price and the customer fit.

Common stock lengths are:

- **16 inches** — closer to the collarbone; a classic pendant length.
- **18 inches** — the most flexible everyday length for many markets.
- **20 inches** — a longer drop, often used for layering or a more relaxed look.

Confirm whether the chain has an extender, how the length is measured, and whether custom lengths are available. If you are planning a layered assortment, keep the lengths distinct so the customer can see a reason to buy more than one piece.

## Match the stone shape to the setting

Moissanite is available in many shapes. The shape affects the setting, the price and the visual style of the necklace.

Common directions include:

- **Heart** — gift and seasonal assortments.
- **Round** — the most flexible shape for halo and bezel settings.
- **Leaf or marquise** — a softer, more directional look.
- **Oval** — a classic pendant silhouette.
- **Emerald cut** — a cleaner, more architectural look.
- **Cluster or station** — multiple stones arranged for a wider visual impact.

For each style, confirm the stone size, the setting type and whether the stone is prong-set, bezel-set or set into a halo. A halo can make a smaller center stone look larger; a bezel can change the perceived color and protect the edge. Neither is automatically better — it depends on the customer and price point.

If you are comparing stone materials, the guide on [moissanite vs cubic zirconia](/resources/moissanite-vs-cubic-zirconia) explains the practical differences.

## Check the clasp and chain construction

The clasp is a small part that causes a large share of customer complaints. Confirm:

- clasp type (lobster clasp, spring ring or other)
- whether the clasp is soldered or attached with a jump ring
- chain width and link style
- whether the chain is adjustable
- how the pendant connects to the chain

A fine chain suits a small pendant; a heavier pendant needs a chain that can carry the weight. Ask for a close-up of the clasp and the pendant connection, not only a front-facing product photo.

## Confirm plating and finish

Most 925 silver moissanite necklaces use rhodium or a gold-tone plating. The finish affects color, care instructions and the perceived price point.

Ask:

- which plating color is standard for the SKU
- whether the plating can be changed for a custom order
- how color consistency is checked across the batch
- what care guidance should be included with the product

Do not promise a specific wear life unless the plating specification and the customer’s use case have been confirmed. A necklace worn daily will age differently from one worn occasionally.

A practical first assortment can include:

Avoid ordering every shape and length at the start. Build around the customer you already have, then reorder the styles that move.

## Build a necklace QC checklist

Before shipment, confirm what will be checked against the approved specification. A practical list includes:

- metal and stamp confirmation
- stone type, size and matching
- setting security and pendant alignment
- chain length and clasp function
- plating color and surface finish
- pendant drop and connection
- logo tag or engraving, if ordered
- packaging and labeling

Ask for photos or a short video of the finished batch, including the clasp and pendant connection.

## Packaging and presentation

Necklaces are often sold on a card or in a box. Confirm whether the order includes cards, pouches, gift boxes or private-label labels. If you need a logo on the card or box, confirm the artwork, minimum quantity and lead time with the necklace order.

For boutiques, a consistent card and pouch can matter as much as the necklace itself. For online sellers, packaging affects the unboxing photo and the return rate.

## A practical quote checklist

When you request a wholesale necklace quote, include:

1. Style reference, SKU or product photo
2. Necklace type (pendant or station)
3. Chain length and whether an extender is needed
4. Stone type, shape, size and color
5. Base metal and plating direction
6. Quantity per style and total order target
7. Logo tag or engraving requirement
8. Packaging requirement
9. Destination country
10. Target delivery window

The clearer the brief, the faster a supplier can confirm stock and quote accurately.
