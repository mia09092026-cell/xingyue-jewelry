Rings are one of the easiest jewelry categories to quote badly. Two rings can look similar in a product photo but differ in metal, stone, setting, plating, size and finishing. For a boutique or online seller, those differences decide whether the ring sells through or comes back as a problem.

This guide covers the details worth confirming before a first wholesale ring order. It is written from the workshop side, not as a price list. Current ready-stock styles are on the [wholesale 925 silver rings page](/wholesale/925-silver-rings).

## Start with the metal and hallmark

“925” means the metal is 92.5% silver. A finished ring may still be rhodium-plated, gold-plated or otherwise finished over the silver, so the buyer should know what the base metal and the surface finish actually are.

Ask for:

- the base metal on the specific SKU
- whether the ring is stamped 925 and where
- the plating color and whether the plating is decorative or functional
- whether the supplier can provide a material statement for the order

Do not assume that “silver tone” means sterling silver. If a listing only says “silver color” or “gold color,” ask for the base metal before comparing price. The same applies to “gold” language: 18K gold-tone plating is not the same product as solid 18K gold.

## Choose the stone direction before comparing price

A ring quote is only comparable when the stone type is clear. Common directions include:

- **Moissanite** — strong fire and a jewelry-focused look, often used for engagement-inspired and gift styles.
- **Cubic zirconia (CZ)** — a lower-cost option for fashion and trial assortments.
- **Lab-grown colored gemstones** — sapphire, ruby, emerald and fancy colors for a more distinctive assortment.
- **Lab-grown diamonds** — usually a higher price point and a different certificate conversation.

Ask how stones are matched across an order. Stone size, cut and color should be reasonably consistent across the pieces in the same size and style. Certificate availability depends on the stone type and order; it should be confirmed rather than assumed.

If you are still deciding between materials, the guide on [moissanite vs cubic zirconia](/resources/moissanite-vs-cubic-zirconia) explains the practical differences.

## Confirm ring sizes and the sizing standard

Ring sizing is a common source of returns. Confirm the size standard used by the supplier, the stock size range and how custom sizes are handled.

For stock orders, common US sizes are the most practical starting point. For a custom run, send a size chart or a measured sample. If you are converting from UK, EU or another system, use a written conversion chart and confirm the final size in the supplier’s own standard.

Useful questions:

- Which sizes are in stock for this style?
- Is the size marked on the ring or only on the packaging?
- What tolerance is acceptable for a custom size?
- Can the band width change without a new mold or sample?

## Understand plating and finish

Plating affects color, durability and the customer’s care instructions. Rhodium is common for a white finish; yellow, rose and white gold tones are also used for fashion assortments.

Ask:

- which plating color is standard for the SKU
- whether plating thickness can be specified
- how the supplier checks color consistency across a batch
- what care guidance should accompany the product

Avoid promising a specific wear life unless the supplier has confirmed the plating specification and the customer’s use case. A ring worn daily will behave differently from a ring worn occasionally.

## Check the setting and finishing details

The setting is where many visual and quality issues appear. For prong settings, check that the stone sits evenly and the prongs are finished. For bezel and halo styles, check the metal around the stone and the consistency of the outline. For eternity or pavé styles, check stone spacing and small-stone matching.

Also review:

- inside-band finish and comfort
- solder joints and component connections
- surface polish and sharp edges
- logo or engraving placement, if ordered

A pre-shipment photo set is useful here. Ask for close-ups of the setting, the stamp and the inside band, not only the front view.

For a first order, a practical mix often includes:

- a few proven solitaire or halo styles
- one or two statement pieces for the top of the price range
- a small size run for the best-selling style
- one or two lower-price styles for entry-level customers

Avoid ordering every style in every size at the start. Build a size curve around your actual customer base and reorder the winners.

## Build a QC checklist

Before shipment, confirm what will be checked against the approved specification. A practical ring QC list includes:

- metal and stamp confirmation
- stone type, size and matching
- setting security and alignment
- ring size and band width
- plating color and surface finish
- logo or engraving placement
- packaging and labeling

Ask for photos or a short video of the finished batch. A quick check is cheaper than a return.

## Packaging and retail presentation

Packaging is part of the product for boutiques and online sellers. Confirm whether the order includes poly bags, pouches, gift boxes, cards or private-label labels. If you need a logo on the card or box, confirm the artwork, minimum quantity and lead time with the ring order, not after production.

## A practical quote checklist

When you request a wholesale ring quote, include:

1. Style reference, SKU or product photo
2. Base metal and plating direction
3. Stone type, size and color
4. Ring size and size curve
5. Quantity per style and total order target
6. Logo or engraving requirement
7. Packaging requirement
8. Destination country
9. Target delivery window
10. Any certificate or testing requirement

The more of these details are clear, the faster a supplier can confirm availability and quote accurately.

## FAQ

### Is 925 sterling silver suitable for rings?

Yes. Sterling silver is a common ring metal. The practical questions are the base metal, the plating, the stone setting and the care guidance. Confirm those details for the specific style.

### Can I mix ring styles in one wholesale order?

Yes, for ready-stock rings. The order minimum is USD 800 total and in-stock styles can be mixed. Custom production starts at 30 pieces per design.

### Can I order custom ring sizes?

Yes. Stock sizes are confirmed per SKU; custom sizes can be produced with a confirmed size chart. Ask about the size standard and tolerance before ordering.

### Do wholesale rings include certificates?

Certificate availability depends on the stone type and the order. Confirm it during quotation rather than assuming that every ring includes a certificate.
